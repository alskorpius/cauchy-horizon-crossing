## 1. Setup

The interior of a regular black hole with two horizons is described by the two-flux model of Ori [1] in the form of Carballo-Rubio et al. [3]: an ingoing Price tail m₋(v) = M − β/v^p (p = 11, β/v₀^p = 10⁻³ M, v₀ = 50 M) and a single outgoing null shell Σ of energy δm₊ = 10⁻⁴ M, which settles on the inner root of f = 1 − 2m/r. The background is the base profile of the inner-extremal family (ℓ = 0.271 M, R₋ = 0.677 M, R₊ = 1.985 M), detuned to κ₋ = −10⁻³/M and −10⁻⁸/M, at the exact triple root κ₋ = 0, and, as the untuned reference, |κ₋| ~ 1/ℓ; Reissner–Nordström (e = 0.9) serves as the control on which Ori's result is reproduced. A test body falls radially with E = 1, entering the hole at advanced time v_e, which equals the external entry time up to an additive constant; the seam (Cauchy horizon) is taken to form at v₀. Along the worldline the tidal tensor in the parallel-propagated frame is E_rad = f_rr/2 and E_⊥ = f_r/(2r) + e^ψ v̇²(∂M/∂v)/r² (Weyl part plus blueshifted ingoing flux; Schwarzschild check −2M/r³, +M/r³). Three quantities are followed: the impulse J = ∫E dτ, Ori's twice-integrated curvature Δ = ∫J dτ (the strain of a free body), and the linear deviation equation ξ″ = −Eξ with ξ = 1, ξ′ = 0 at the outer horizon (the actual size of a free body). Crossing Σ delivers a transverse impulse I_sh = [M]|u̇|/R² → E/(ρR), where [M] is the Misner–Sharp jump across the shell and 1/ρ = f₊/f₋ is the blueshift of the region behind it; the radial impulse vanishes for a null shell.

The seam acts impulsively: the proper time from the shell to the classical end is τ₊ ~ ρR/E, so the relevant criterion for a bound body of size L is the relative velocity I_sh·L imparted across it against its binding, not a strain. The thresholds are: molecular bonds, 10⁹ Pa at 10³ kg m⁻³ → v_b = 1.4 km s⁻¹ across 1 m; nuclei, 8 MeV per nucleon → v_b = 0.14 c across 1 fm (a strain criterion is meaningless at this scale); Planck, Kretschmann scalar on the R₊ side of the shell ≥ 1/l_P⁴ (end of the classical description). The strain criterion Δ ≥ 10⁻² is exceeded by every crossing through the background field alone (|Δ| = 0.7…7 for a free body), and the quasi-static core stress ρ_body E L² with E ~ 1/ℓ² reproduces the usual spaghettification hierarchy (5.6·10¹² Pa for 1 m at 10 M☉ down to 5.6·10⁻⁶ Pa at 10⁹ M☉), independent of the seam.

## 2. Two regimes of the crossing

Early entrants (1/ρ ≈ 1, I_sh ≪ 1) meet the shell away from R₋ (r_cross = 1.31 M at v_e = v₀) and then traverse the repulsive de Sitter core, where E_rad = E_⊥ = −1/ℓ²: a free body is stretched, ξ_⊥ = 23 and Δ_⊥ = −7.2 at the first crossing. This is the cost of the interior itself, not of the seam; it grows with the amplitude of the ingoing tail only because a heavier tail lowers the inner root and lengthens the core passage.

Late entrants (I_sh ≫ 1) are kicked at the shell into a radially converging family and reach the classical end within τ₊ ~ ρR/E. Their accumulated deformation converges to a universal value: Δ_⊥ → 1.01, ξ_⊥ → 3·10⁻³ (transverse compression to 0.3 % of the initial size), Δ_rad → 0.26, ξ_rad → 0.74 — the same for κ₋ = −10⁻³, −10⁻⁸ and 0, and 1.04 / 3.4·10⁻³ / 0.16 / 0.85 for Reissner–Nordström. This is the single-shell counterpart of Ori's statement that the mass-inflation singularity is weak: the first integral J ~ 1/ρ diverges while the second, Δ ~ J τ₊, stays finite (RN: shell growth d ln Q/dv = 1.1668, 1.2352, 1.3096 at v = 60, 90, 200 against Ori's |κ| − (p+1)/v = 1.1698, 1.2365, 1.3098). What grows without bound with the entry time is the impulse: for κ₋ ≠ 0, 1/ρ ∝ e^{|κ₋| v_e} with doubling time ln 2/|κ₋| (693 M for κ₋ = −10⁻³/M, 6.9·10⁷ M for −10⁻⁸/M, and 0.19 M = 0.69 ℓ/c only for the untuned profile); for the exact triple root 1/ρ ∝ v_e^{3/2} (the hovering shell of fixed energy 2.3·10⁻⁵ M is blueshifted by 1/|f₋| ∝ v^{3/2}). The mass jump [M] and the curvature on the shell side stay at their initial values until the exponential phase catches up (v_e ~ 10⁵ M for −10⁻³, ~3·10¹⁰ M for −10⁻⁸, never for κ₋ = 0, where K on the shell stays at the background 19 M⁻⁴ for all epochs).

The starting cost is finite and non-zero: I_sh(v₀) = 4.2·10⁻⁴ M⁻¹ × (δm₊/10⁻⁴), i.e. 8.6 s⁻¹ for 10 M☉ (a 1 m body receives 8.6 m s⁻¹ of transverse relative velocity, a 10 km body 86 km s⁻¹) and 8.6·10⁻⁸ s⁻¹ for 10⁹ M☉. It is linear in the energy of the outgoing shell (the back-scattered tail) and changes by only 4 % when the ingoing amplitude runs from 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻²; v₀ = 20, 50, 100 M change it by less than 0.1 %.

Table 1. Entry time after seam formation at which a body of the given size loses the given level of structure when it crosses the seam; the classical description ends at the Planck column.

Read for one cell: a 10⁹ M☉ hole with κ₋ = −10⁻³/M lets bodies entering within the first ~3 years after the seam forms cross it with molecular structure intact at the metre scale, those entering within ~10 years with nuclei intact; after ~51 years the seam is trans-Planckian and the crossing has no classical description. For stellar-mass holes the molecular window is milliseconds or absent, the nuclear window seconds, and the classical description ends within a minute; the untuned profile (|κ₋| ~ 1/ℓ) shortens every window by two to three orders of magnitude relative to the 10⁻³ detuning. Figure 1 shows the same table as time lines.

The exact triple root deserves its own line. Its seam never becomes trans-Planckian and its curvature does not grow, but the impulse still grows as v_e^{3/2}: a free metre-sized body is crushed transversally at every epoch later than ~10⁴ M (0.5 s for 10 M☉, 1.6 yr for 10⁹ M☉), molecular bonds break after 10 ms (10 M☉) … 3·10³ yr (10⁹ M☉) — the same molecular window as for the detuned profiles, because it is set by the v^{3/2} phase that precedes any exponential growth — while nuclei survive for ~10³ yr at 10 M☉ and far beyond the age of the universe for heavier holes.

Conclusion. Regularity of the core means finiteness of the curvature on the seam, but not softness of the crossing: what grows with the entry time is the velocity kick, not the deformation. The accumulated deformation of a free body is finite and epoch-independent for every profile examined (Ori's weak singularity); the passable window is set by the impulse and is measured in milliseconds for stellar-mass holes and in years to millennia for supermassive ones.

## 4. Limitations

Test body without back-reaction; classical treatment, with the Planck column only marking where it ends. Single outgoing shell: the entry time acts only through 1/ρ and [M] at the moment the observer meets the shell, and "crossing the seam" is identified with entering the inflated region behind it; the u-dependence of a continuous outflux (Poisson–Israel [2]) is checked separately [T35A]: replacing the single shell by N = 3…1000 outgoing shells discretising a u⁻¹² outflux changes the deformation at the classical end by at most 2·10⁻⁴ (detuned profiles, RN) and 3·10⁻³ (exact triple root), with Δ converging in N and staying epoch-independent — the single-shell numbers quoted here are therefore not an artefact of the idealisation. Behind the shell the mass law is the linearised family law; the exact ℓ-fixed family would put the Planck cells earlier by at most a few e-folds and leaves the impulse and the pre-inflation numbers unchanged. Model parameters: ingoing amplitude 10⁻³ (10⁻⁴…10⁻²: 4 % effect on the impulse), outgoing-shell energy 10⁻⁴ (10⁻⁵…10⁻³: the starting impulse is linear in it), p = 11, v₀ = 50 M; the E = 1 radial observer; the deviation equation is linear (ξ → 0 means "compressed beyond the linear regime"). The base-profile tuning residue was removed with a local window so that κ₋ = 0 is an exact triple root (a = f‴/6 = −2.169).

## 5. Relation to the literature

Ori [1] showed on an exact mass-inflation solution that the Cauchy-horizon singularity is weak: "its tidal gravitational forces do not necessarily destroy physical objects which attempt to cross it" (abstract verified in T34). Poisson and Israel [2] established the infinite blueshift of the radiative tail and the unbounded mass function (SOURCES 102). The present result adds two things: the finite, universal deformation is reproduced numerically for a regular (inner-extremal) profile, including the exact triple root; and the qualitative statement "weak, finite tides" is replaced by a window table in external time with molecular, nuclear and Planck thresholds. To our knowledge no such table exists in the literature (searched, not found; the absence is not asserted).

Paper 1 (preprint/main.tex) carries one claim — existence, sensitivity and the absence of classical selection of the inner-extremal family — and its appendices are method appendices (KS derivation, thin walls, rotation caveat, numerics); a crossing-window table would be a second claim with its own model (two-flux Ori, test body, thresholds), its own figure and its own limitations, and would need the continuous-outflux check of T35A before it can be stated. As a separate note of 4–5 pages (this section plus Figure 1, Table 1 and the RN control) it can cite paper 1 for the family and stand or fall on its own; if T35A confirms convergence, a one-sentence forward reference in paper 1's Discussion ("the crossing of the degenerate inner horizon is analysed in [note]") is enough.
